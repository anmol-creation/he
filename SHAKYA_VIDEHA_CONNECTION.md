# Historical and Geographical Connection: Shakyas (Kapilavastu) and Videha (Mithila)

This document preserves the discussion and hypothesis regarding the connection between the Buddhist Shakya lineage and the Hindu Puranic lineage of Nimi (Videha/Mithila).

## 1. The Tale of Two Branches (Ikshvaku's Descendants)
A major historiographical puzzle exists in mapping the descendants of King Ikshvaku between Hindu Puranas and Buddhist traditions. A highly logical approach is to view the divergent names as **separate branches stemming from the same ancestor**.

*   **Hindu Puranic Tradition:** States King Ikshvaku had 100 sons. The most prominent were:
    *   **Vikukshi:** Founded the main Ayodhya line (leading to Dasharatha and Rama).
    *   **Nimi:** Traveled east/north and founded the kingdom of Videha (Mithila), leading to the Janaka dynasty.
*   **Buddhist Tradition:** States King Okkaka (Ikshvaku) banished his elder children to ensure the throne for his favored queen's son.
    *   **Okkamukha:** The eldest exiled son, who traveled to the Himalayan foothills and founded the Shakya clan.

**Hypothesis:** It is highly probable that **Okkamukha** (Buddhist texts) and **Nimi** (Hindu texts) represent branches of the same family (the "100 sons" of Ikshvaku) migrating to the same geographical region (the Himalayan Terai).

## 2. Geographical Proximity
Geography strongly supports this connection.
*   **Kapilavastu (Shakya Republic):** Located in the Himalayan foothills (modern-day Indo-Nepal border, Terai region).
*   **Mithila (Videha Kingdom):** Located just to the east, also along the Himalayan foothills (North Bihar / Nepal border).
Both kingdoms share the exact same ecological and geographical belt, originating from the same solar lineage at approximately the same time.

## 3. Sage Kapila: The Cross-Tradition Link
A rare point of perfect agreement between Hindu, Buddhist, and Tibetan texts:
*   **Hindu Puranas:** Kapilavastu was named after Sage Kapila (founder of Sankhya philosophy, an avatar of Vishnu), whose ashram was located there.
*   **Buddhist/Tibetan Texts (e.g., Dulva, Ambattha Sutta):** Explicitly state that Okkamukha and his siblings met the great sage **Kapila** in the Himalayan foothills. He permitted them to build a city near his ashram, which they named **Kapilavastu** in his honor.

## 4. Synthesis of Names and Traditions
*   **The Paradox of Shibi:** The legendary King Shibi is claimed by Puranas as Chandravanshi (Anu lineage), while Chola inscriptions (South India) and Buddhist texts place him in the Suryavanshi (Ikshvaku/Mandhata) line. This illustrates how ancient 'Ideal Kings' were claimed by different traditions.
*   **Linguistic Shifts:** Sanskrit names perfectly translate to Pali equivalents, proving they speak of the same historical figures:
    *   Ikshvaku -> Okkaka
    *   Mandhata -> Mandhata
    *   Rama -> Ramapandita
    *   Shibi/Sivi -> Sivi
    *   Dasharatha -> Dasaratha

**Conclusion:** By treating differing narratives not as contradictions, but as isolated regional memories of different branches (e.g., Ayodhya vs. Mithila vs. Kapilavastu), we can build a cohesive, unified historical map.
